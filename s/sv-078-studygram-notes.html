Studygram Notes

SV-078

Example: The data science lifecycle, set in Studygram Notes

In plain words

A page that looks like a top student's handwritten study notes on cream grid paper - bold hand-lettered headings with a marker underline, small hand-drawn charts with arrows, and neat sections split by dashed lines. It feels personal, organised and easy to learn from. What makes it this style is that colour is used for navigation, each colour meaning one kind of idea, not decoration. Best for: cheatsheets, explainers, handouts and pre-reads that teach a method step by step. Avoid: posters and anything that must feel authoritative from across a room - handwriting reads informal at a distance, and a comic or childish hand kills the rigour.

Topics it fits

  • Course handouts and cheatsheetsthe grid-paper notebook look makes dense material feel like a friendly private study file
  • Explaining a formula or metrichand-drawn axis diagrams with arrows show what a slope or area means beside the text that names it
  • Corporate AI training readerscolour-as-navigation, one colour per idea type, lowers the load when a reader returns to the page
  • Personal learning notes in publicthe studygram look shows a learner's own process, which suits sharing what you are studying

Art style and layout

  • core locked [grid_paper_ground, hand_lettered_headings, hand_drawn_axis_diagrams, colour_as_navigation, marker_highlight, dashed_dividers]
  • medium digital pen notes on grid paper; inline SVG diagrams with a hand stroke
  • ground cream-white paper with a fine light-grey 5 mm grid (light only, never dark)
  • headings hand-lettered, bold pen, numbered sections (一、二、三 (Chinese numerals) / 1, 2, 3); the title carries a coloured marker underline
  • body neat handwriting or a clean hand-feel font; formulas set inline
  • diagrams right-floating, hand-drawn axes with arrowheads, one curve or shape, hatching for areas, a dot at the key point, short hand labels with curved leader arrows
  • colour roles (source) ink #2b2b2b; moss green = sensitivity / change; brick red = risk / probability area; warm brown = summary; soft orange-brown marker highlight behind key phrases
  • dividers long dashed hairlines between modules
  • decoration at most one small doodle in the title corner (a coin, a star)
  • the tension rigorous content in a warm personal hand. If it turns cute or childish, the rigour is lost; if the hand is too neat, it is just another template

What must stay, what may change

Swap the subject freely. Keep the grid-paper ground, hand-lettered headings with a marker underline, hand-drawn axis diagrams with leader arrows, colour-as-navigation, marker highlights and dashed dividers between left-text / right-diagram modules.
