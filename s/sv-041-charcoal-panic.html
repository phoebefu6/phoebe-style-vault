Charcoal Panic

SV-041

No finished piece in this style yet - the bands are its palette.

In plain words

A big, dark, scratchy charcoal creature, chest-up and staring straight out, on a loud red background with a short panicked caption like OH NO in a corner. Only three colours appear and there is no lighting at all, so it reads as a grave drawing of a very small feeling. The style is the funny clash between a heavy dark mass and a tiny emotion.

Topics it fits

  • Production incident post-mortemsa huge dark creature mouthing OH NO captures the moment a dashboard breaks with humour that disarms blame
  • Change fatiguethe AGAIN caption on a deadpan beast names the shared feeling of yet another reorg
  • LinkedIn hooksthree colours and a one-word panic caption stop the scroll and read at thumbnail size
  • Workshop icebreakersmatching each team mood to a creature gives people a safe, funny way to name how they feel

Art style and layout

  • core locked [three_colour_ceiling, no_light_at_all, broken_charcoal_edge, oversize_eyes_tiny_pupils, hand_set_caption]
  • medium dry charcoal and soft graphite on toothy paper, flattened - matte, no gloss, no digital gradient, no airbrush
  • three colour ceiling SIGNATURE. Ground + charcoal + cream. A fourth colour collapses it into ordinary children's-book illustration instantly. This is the rule that does the most work
  • broken charcoal edge no contour line exists. The silhouette is built from hundreds of short directional scratches and stays fringed and porous; the red ground reads *through* the fur at the perimeter. A clean vector edge kills the style dead
  • eye treatment two oversized round bone-cream discs, each with a small off-centre dot pupil, set wide and slightly high. Eye area is the only part of the frame with a hard clean edge - the contrast against the fringe is the whole face
  • expression economy the entire emotion is carried by pupil size, pupil position and one small curved mouth. No brows, no cheeks, no blush, no sweat drops, no motion lines
  • light NONE. State it explicitly - MJ adds a rim light and a cast shadow by default and both instantly turn it into a render
  • palette [vermilion "#DD2E22, charcoal "#0E0B0A, bone cream "#F3E9D6]
  • ground rule the background is one unbroken saturated field with nothing in it - no texture, no vignette, no second object. The emptiness is what makes the caption land
  • typography hand-lettered caps, brush or soft marker, two short lines, wobbly baseline, generous letter-spacing, one punctuation mark carrying the tone. Cream on the ground colour. Set at 8-14% of frame height, upper-left, never touching the creature. Design-build only
  • caption rule two or three words maximum, and it must be a REACTION, not a label. "OH NO!" works; "BLACK CAT" is dead
  • composition square 1:1. Roughly a third of the frame stays as empty ground. Whiskers may cross into that space and should - they stitch the two zones together
  • negative space load-bearing, unlike SV-028 which packs edge to edge. Here the empty colour field is a co-star
  • hierarchy the eyes land first, the caption second, the silhouette mass third, the whisker scratches last
  • mood deadpan comic panic - grave drawing, trivial emotion
  • era reference contemporary picture-book illustration and single-panel newspaper gag drawing. No living artist named in generation prompts

What must stay, what may change

emotion + creature paired, ten far-apart registers (OH NO / MINE / AGAIN? / I KNEW IT / WHO, ME?) each on a different animal family - NOT ten cats. The source frame was a cat; that is the trap, same as the 2026-08-12 moth set
